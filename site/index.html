Evidence, not a project

The compliance work is already done, because it is how the product works.

Article 12 asks for automatic, traceable records over the system's life. Article 14 asks you to show that a human can understand, intervene and override. Both became enforceable on 2 August 2026.

Tacit's log is append-only and sealed into a hash chain, so an altered or deleted record is detectable by anyone holding an earlier root. One click produces the bundle: every system in scope, its autonomy level, the oversight that applies, the approvals, the refusals, the reversals — including for the third-party agents you supervise.

Everybody bought observability. Nobody owns whether the work was right.

Other tools score an AI against a prompt or a panel of judges. Tacit scores it against what your colleague actually did, in the same thread, minutes later. There is nothing to label and nothing to maintain.

03

Pricing that can't be gamed

Per-resolution pricing bills you when the chat ends. Verified-work pricing bills only when a human approved the action or let it stand. Reversed work is credited back, permanently.

04

Autonomy per job, earned

05

Reversible by construction

Every write records its inverse. Undo a run across Slack, GitHub and files in one click — and the undo is on the audit log too.

06

Cover when someone's out

See which jobs live in one person's head. When they go on leave, promote their proven jobs for a week with approvals routed to a backup, then restore them automatically.

FAQ

Questions worth asking

How can you grade another vendor's agent without their cooperation?

You already have the record: the messages, comments and tickets it produced sit in your own tools. Tacit reads those as events — the same read-only feed it uses for humans — and compares them with what your team does in the same situation. The vendor is not involved and nothing on their side changes.

Isn't "similarity to what a human wrote" a crude score?

On its own, yes. That's why the headline metric is rework: did a person have to step in afterwards? That is a fact, not a judgement. Similarity explains why, and anyone can override a grade with one tap.

What stops you billing for work that looks fine but isn't?

The dispute window and the undo button. Nothing is billed while a human can still reverse it, and reversing it credits the line permanently. You can also read every line and see the exact reason it was billed.

Does it need a model?

No. The local brain drafts from your team's own past replies and runs a deterministic planner, so mining, shadow scoring, oversight, approvals, undo and the audit log all work with no key at all. Add Claude when you want drafts that adapt to context.

Where does the data live?

On your infrastructure, in one database — SQLite or Postgres. Secrets are environment variables, never rows. One API call exports everything, including the chain root.
