How a job gets hired

A Tuesday with Tacit.

Priya works in support. Twelve times a month someone asks “where's the invoice for Acme?” and she answers the same way. Nobody wrote that job down. Here is how Tacit takes it over — and only that job.

01 · WATCH

It notices the job exists

You connect Slack. Tacit reads what happens and spots that this question → this reply recurs, from Priya, quickly, consistently. It writes it up as a playbook with the evidence attached.

CANDIDATE  Answer “invoice” threads in #billing · priya · seen 14× · usually answered within 1.0h · consistency 78%
02 · SHADOW

It practises in secret

You click “shadow”. From now on, every time someone asks for an invoice, Tacit writes the reply Priya would write — and files it where nobody sees it. Two hours later Priya replies for real. Tacit compares the two and scores itself.

similarity 0.81 · HIT · trust 13/15 → 62%
03 · BACKTEST

You get the number on day one

You don't have to wait weeks. Tacit replays the same shadow process over the last 90 days of history — drafting blind, scoring against the replies that already happened — and shows you every hit and every miss.

Backtest · Answer “invoice” threads   13 of 15 historical triggers matched · mean score 89 · 0.2s   WOULD HAVE HANDLED 87%
04 · PROPOSE

It asks for permission — each time

When the score clears the bar, Tacit recommends promotion. Now invoice questions land in your Inbox as a finished reply with a preview of exactly what will be posted. One tap approves. Anything that looks unlike the pattern is flagged as an outlier and escalated even later, on auto.

05 · AUTO

It takes over — with an undo button

After enough approvals and no rejections, it asks to go fully automatic for this one job. It runs under a budget, every action is logged with its inverse, and three undos demote it back to asking. When Priya misses a draft, Tacit asks her one question and the answer becomes a rule.

Why it's different

Other AI teammates ask for trust on day one.
Tacit earns it, in public.

01

Self-supervised trust

No labelling, no eval sets. Every draft is graded against the human's real action. Trust is a Wilson lower bound, so ten lucky hits don't count as proof.

02

Autonomy per job, not per bot

Shadow → propose → auto, each job on its own ladder, with demotion when undos or rejections pile up. You always know exactly what it may do unattended.

03

Outliers get escalated

A trigger that doesn't resemble anything the job was learned from is routed to a human — even on auto. Confidence is shown on every draft.

04

Reversible by construction

Comments know how to delete themselves; file writes keep the previous content. Undo a run across GitHub, Slack and files in one click, and the undo is audited too.

05

It learns from corrections

When a draft misses, Tacit asks the owner one question. The answer becomes a rule that conditions every future draft of that job.

06

Cover when someone's out

See which jobs live only in one person's head. When they're on leave, promote their proven jobs to propose for a week — with approvals routed to a backup.

Replay

A regression suite you never had to write.

Every run is a recording: the input, the decisions, the tool results. Replay any of them against a new model or prompt with recorded results substituted in — nothing touches the outside world — and diff the decisions. Upgrading the model becomes a measurement, not a leap of faith.

Security & control

Built for the people who say no.

Everything a security review asks about is a first-class object with an API, not a slide.

Self-hosted

One process, one SQLite or Postgres database, on your machine or in your VPC. Your context never leaves unless you connect a cloud model.

Permissions as rules

Every tool carries a risk class. Rules map (principal, tool) → allow, ask or deny. Denied tools aren't even offered to the model.

Hard budgets

Writes per hour and dollars per day per principal pattern. Enforced before execution; no model can talk its way past them.

Append-only audit

Who asked, what ran, what changed, whether it worked — including undos and stage changes. Export as JSONL to your SIEM.

Secrets stay secrets

Tokens live in the environment, never in the database. API keys are hashed; sessions are signed cookies; passwords use scrypt.

Leave in one call

GET /api/v1/export returns every table as JSON. No export request, no retention window, no lock-in.

FAQ

Questions worth asking

Isn't “similarity to what the human wrote” a crude score?

It's an explainable one, and it's calibrated against the only thing that matters: would the team have accepted this? Every score sits next to the draft and the real reply. A human can override any grade with one tap, and misses turn into questions the owner answers in a sentence.

What about jobs that need judgement?

They score badly in shadow, which is the point: Tacit recommends leaving them alone, or retiring the playbook. And on the jobs it does take, triggers that don't resemble the pattern are escalated to a person — even on auto.

Does it need a model?

No. The local brain drafts from your team's own past replies and runs a deterministic planner, so the entire harness works without a key. Add Claude when you want drafts that adapt to context.

How is this different from process mining or RPA?

Process mining tells you a pattern exists. RPA replays clicks. Tacit does the knowledge-work version — replies, triage, reviews, summaries — and proves each job in shadow against the human before it is allowed to act.

Where does the data live?

On your infrastructure, in one database. Secrets are environment variables, never rows. One API call exports everything.
